How an EU law is made

Follow the ordinary legislative procedure from proposal to adoption.

On this page
UnderstandExplorePracticeExplain itKey takeawaysSources

UNDERSTAND

The ordinary legislative procedure in one sentence

In the most common EU law-making procedure, the Commission proposes and the European Parliament and Council of the EU examine, amend and must agree before a legislative act can be adopted.

1ProposalCommission
→
2ExaminationParliament + Council
→
3AgreementSame final text
→
4AdoptionEU legislative act

Real files can involve readings, amendments, negotiations and — if needed — conciliation. The crucial idea is not to memorise every branch first. It is to know who initiates, who co-legislates and why agreement matters.

EXPLORE

Put the core steps in order

  • Parliament and Council agree on the same text
  • Commission presents a legislative proposal
  • The agreed act is adopted and can be published
  • Parliament and Council examine the proposal

EXPLAIN IT

Explain it without reciting a flowchart

“How would you explain the EU’s ordinary legislative procedure to someone unfamiliar with the institutions?”

  1. Commission proposal.
  2. Parliament and Council act as co-legislators.
  3. Both examine and can amend.
  4. Adoption requires agreement on a common text.
  5. Mention that the detailed procedure can include several readings and conciliation.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

The mental model

  • Initiative: generally Commission.
  • Co-legislators: Parliament + Council of the EU.
  • Agreement is the central constraint.
  • The European Council gives political direction but is not a normal co-legislator.
