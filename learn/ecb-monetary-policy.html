How ECB interest rates reach the economy

Trace the transmission mechanism instead of memorising a one-line slogan.

On this page
UnderstandExplorePracticeExplain itKey takeawaysSources

UNDERSTAND

A policy rate decision is only the first link in a chain

The ECB’s primary objective is price stability. Changes in its key interest rates influence financing conditions across the euro area. Those conditions affect borrowing, saving, investment and spending; these changes influence economic activity and, with lags, inflation.

1ECB ratesPolicy stance
→
2Market & bank ratesFinancing conditions
→
3Spending & investmentDemand
→
4InflationWith lags

That chain is not mechanical. Expectations, bank balance sheets, financial markets, global conditions and the source of inflation all matter. A good answer therefore explains the mechanism without pretending the effect is instantaneous or certain.

EXPLAIN IT

Interview answer framework

“How do higher ECB interest rates affect the real economy?”

  1. Policy rates influence money-market and broader financing conditions.
  2. Bank lending and market borrowing become more expensive; saving incentives can rise.
  3. Consumption and investment may weaken.
  4. Lower demand can reduce price pressures.
  5. Mention expectations, exchange rates/financial conditions and time lags as additional channels or qualifications.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

Focus on the full transmission mechanism

  • Policy rates are an instrument, not the final outcome.
  • Transmission works through financing conditions and behaviour.
  • Effects are delayed and uncertain.
  • The inflation target is 2% over the medium term and symmetric.
