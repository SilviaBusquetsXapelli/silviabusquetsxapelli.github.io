Inflation without the fog

Understand the rate, the price level and why the source of inflation matters.

On this page
UnderstandExplorePracticeExplain itKey takeawaysSources

UNDERSTAND

Inflation is a rate of change not a price level

Inflation describes how the overall price level of a basket of goods and services changes over time. A country can have high prices with low current inflation, or lower prices with high current inflation. That distinction matters.

Euro-area measure

The ECB assesses price stability using the Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices (HICP) and aims for 2% inflation over the medium term.

Why the cause matters

Inflation can reflect strong demand, supply constraints, energy shocks, wage and cost dynamics, expectations, exchange-rate movements or combinations of these. The same inflation number can therefore imply different economic trade-offs.

EXPLAIN IT

60 second interview practice

“Inflation has fallen sharply. Why might households still feel that prices are high?”

  1. Inflation is the rate of price increase, not the price level.
  2. Lower inflation means prices may be rising more slowly.
  3. Past increases remain embedded in the price level unless there is deflation.
  4. Households experience different personal baskets, so perceived inflation can differ from the aggregate index.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

Keep these distinctions

  • Inflation ≠ price level.
  • Disinflation ≠ deflation.
  • Aggregate inflation ≠ every price moving by the same amount.
  • Diagnosis matters for policy.
