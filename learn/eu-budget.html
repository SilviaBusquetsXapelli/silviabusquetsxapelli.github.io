How the EU budget works

Separate the multi-year framework from the annual budget and see where the institutions fit.

On this page
UnderstandExplorePracticeExplain itKey takeawaysSources

UNDERSTAND

Two layers: long-term framework and annual budget

The EU plans spending within a multi-year ceiling called the Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF). Inside that framework, an annual budget is negotiated and adopted for each financial year.

LONG TERM

MFF

Sets multi-year spending priorities and maximum amounts by broad category.

YEAR BY YEAR

Annual budget

Allocates revenue and expenditure for a specific financial year within the long-term framework.

The Commission prepares proposals and implements the budget; Parliament and the Council play central roles in negotiation and adoption. The exact legal rules differ between the long-term framework and the annual budget.

EXPLAIN IT

A useful public-finance answer

“What is the difference between the EU’s MFF and its annual budget?”

  1. MFF = multi-year framework, priorities and ceilings.
  2. Annual budget = one-year allocation within that framework.
  3. Connect the institutions: Commission proposes/drafts; Parliament and Council negotiate/adopt under the relevant procedures.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

What to keep straight

  • MFF is the long-term framework, not the annual budget.
  • The annual budget sits inside the MFF.
  • Budget decisions involve institutional bargaining as well as accounting.
  • Implementation and scrutiny are separate from adoption.
