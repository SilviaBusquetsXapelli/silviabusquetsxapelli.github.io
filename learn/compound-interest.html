What is compound interest?

Compounding means returns can themselves begin generating returns over time.

On this page
UnderstandExplorePracticeExplain itKey takeawaysSources

UNDERSTAND

Growth on earlier growth

With compounding, each period starts from a new balance. Time therefore matters: the same rate applied for longer can create a widening gap compared with simple interest.

Useful distinction

For a single initial amount with annual compounding, a basic formula is A = P(1+r)^t. Real products may compound differently and may include fees or taxes.

EXPLORE

Try the formula

FormulaA = P(1 + r)t

P = starting amount · r = annual rate as a decimal · t = years. Educational illustration; real products can use different compounding, fees and taxes.

PRACTICE

Check the idea

If €1,000 earns 5% for one year, the next year’s 5% is calculated on what amount?

Common mistake: “A higher advertised return automatically means a better investment.”

Return has to be considered alongside risk, fees, inflation, taxes and the product’s actual terms.

EXPLAIN IT

60 second interview practice

“Why can a small difference in an interest rate matter so much over many years?”

Reveal answer structure

Because each period's growth can become part of the base for the next period. Over long horizons, repeated compounding means differences in the rate and time can produce increasingly different outcomes.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

Compounding means returns can themselves begin generating returns over time.

Use the distinction above when you meet this concept in news, data or a real-world decision. The goal here is to understand the mechanism — not to memorise a definition.
