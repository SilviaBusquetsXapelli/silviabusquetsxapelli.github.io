What is an ETF?

An exchange-traded fund pools assets while its shares trade on an exchange.

On this page
UnderstandExplorePracticeExplain itKey takeawaysSources

UNDERSTAND

One vehicle, potentially many holdings

An ETF can hold shares, bonds or other assets according to a stated strategy or index. Buying one ETF can therefore give exposure to many underlying securities.

Useful distinction

ETF does not automatically mean diversified, low-risk or cheap. What matters is what the fund holds, how concentrated it is, its costs and its structure.

PRACTICE

Check the idea

Which question tells you most about an ETF’s diversification?

Common mistake: “Every ETF is broadly diversified.”

Some ETFs are very concentrated in one sector, country, theme or even a small number of holdings.

EXPLAIN IT

60 second interview practice

“Is an ETF automatically diversified and low-risk?”

Reveal answer structure

No. An ETF is a fund structure traded on an exchange. What matters is what the fund actually holds: an ETF can track a broad market, a narrow sector, one country or another concentrated exposure.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

An exchange-traded fund pools assets while its shares trade on an exchange.

Use the distinction above when you meet this concept in news, data or a real-world decision. The goal here is to understand the mechanism — not to memorise a definition.
