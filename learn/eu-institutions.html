Who does what in the EU?

A practical map of the institutions and the roles you need to keep separate.

On this page
UnderstandExplorePracticeExplain itKey takeawaysSources

UNDERSTAND

Start with the four institutions that shape most EU decisions

The names are similar, but their jobs are not. A useful first map is: the European Commission generally proposes legislation and promotes the EU’s general interest; the European Parliament represents EU citizens; the Council of the European Union represents national governments; and the European Council brings together EU leaders to set the Union’s overall political direction.

01

European Commission

Develops policy initiatives, generally proposes new EU legislation and helps ensure EU law is applied.

02

European Parliament

Directly elected. Shares legislative and budgetary functions with the Council of the EU.

03

Council of the EU

National ministers meet by policy area. It shares legislative and budgetary functions with Parliament.

04

European Council

Heads of state or government set broad political direction and priorities; it does not normally legislate.

The common trap

European Council ≠ Council of the European Union ≠ Council of Europe. The Council of Europe is a separate international organisation, not an EU institution.

EXPLAIN IT

60-second interview practice

“Can you briefly explain the difference between the European Council and the Council of the European Union?”

  1. Start with membership: leaders versus national ministers.
  2. State the function: political direction versus legislative/policy coordination.
  3. Finish with the key distinction: the European Council does not normally legislate; the Council of the EU does.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

What to remember

  • Commission: generally proposes and implements.
  • Parliament: citizens; directly elected; co-legislator.
  • Council of the EU: national governments; co-legislator.
  • European Council: national leaders; broad direction and priorities.
