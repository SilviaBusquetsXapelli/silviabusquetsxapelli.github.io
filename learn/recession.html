What is a recession?

A recession is a broad decline in economic activity, not simply one weak company or one bad week.

On this page
UnderstandExplorePracticeExplain itKey takeawaysSources

UNDERSTAND

Look for breadth, depth and duration

Economists examine several indicators — output, employment, income and spending — because an economy is too complex to diagnose from one number alone.

Useful distinction

Two consecutive quarters of falling real GDP is a common rule of thumb, but it is not a universal definition of recession.

EXPLORE

Think about it

GDP falls slightly for one quarter while employment and household income continue to rise. Is “recession” automatic?

Make a prediction before you open the quick check below.

PRACTICE

Check the idea

GDP falls slightly for one quarter while employment and household income continue to rise. Is “recession” automatic?

Common mistake: “A recession simply means the stock market fell.”

Financial markets can move before, during or without a recession. A recession concerns broad economic activity.

EXPLAIN IT

60 second interview practice

“GDP fell in one quarter. Can we already say the economy is in recession?”

Not necessarily. A recession describes a broad decline in economic activity. Analysts look at several indicators and at the depth, duration and spread of weakness rather than relying on one isolated number.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

A recession is a broad decline in economic activity, not simply one weak company or one bad week.

Use the distinction above when you meet this concept in news, data or a real-world decision. The goal here is to understand the mechanism — not to memorise a definition.
