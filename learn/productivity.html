What is productivity?

Productivity asks how much output can be produced from a given amount of input.

On this page
UnderstandExplorePracticeExplain itKey takeawaysSources

UNDERSTAND

Doing more with the same resources

Labour productivity is often measured as output per hour worked or per worker. It can rise through better tools, skills, organisation, technology and capital.

Useful distinction

Productivity is not the same as “working harder”. A better process can raise output while reducing wasted effort.

EXPLORE

Think about it

A team makes 80 units in 10 hours, then improves its process and makes 100 units in 10 hours. What changed?

Make a prediction before you open the quick check below.

QUICK CHECK

Check the idea

A team makes 80 units in 10 hours, then improves its process and makes 100 units in 10 hours. What changed?

Common mistake: “Higher productivity always means longer working hours.”

Productivity is output relative to inputs. Better methods can increase it without longer hours.

EXPLAIN IT

Can you explain it without the jargon?

Imagine a friend has never studied this topic. Explain the main idea in two sentences, then give one everyday example. If you need to use a technical word, explain that word too.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

Productivity asks how much output can be produced from a given amount of input.

Use the distinction above when you meet this concept in news, data or a real-world decision. The goal here is to understand the mechanism — not to memorise a definition.
