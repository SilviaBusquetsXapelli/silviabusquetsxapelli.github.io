What is a stock?

A stock or share represents an ownership interest in a company.

On this page
UnderstandExplorePracticeExplain itKey takeawaysSources

UNDERSTAND

A small slice of ownership

Shareholders can benefit if a company becomes more valuable and may receive dividends when declared. But the market price can fall and dividends are not guaranteed.

Useful distinction

Owning a share is different from lending to the company. A bond is debt; a share is equity ownership.

EXPLORE

Think about it

A company has 1,000 equal shares and you own 10. Ignoring different share classes, what fraction do you own?

Make a prediction before you open the quick check below.

PRACTICE

Check the idea

A company has 1,000 equal shares and you own 10. Ignoring different share classes, what fraction do you own?

Common mistake: “A share price rising means the company has paid you that gain in cash.”

A market gain is unrealised until you sell; prices can move again before then.

EXPLAIN IT

60 second interview practice

“If I buy one share, what exactly do I own?”

A share represents a small ownership interest in a company. Its market price can rise or fall, and shareholder rights vary, but buying a share is different from lending money to the company.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

A stock or share represents an ownership interest in a company.

Use the distinction above when you meet this concept in news, data or a real-world decision. The goal here is to understand the mechanism — not to memorise a definition.
